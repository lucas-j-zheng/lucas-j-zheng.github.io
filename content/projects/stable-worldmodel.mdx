An independent, two-month research extension of [Stable World Models](https://github.com/galilai-group/stable-worldmodel), developed in more than 130 authored commits and run on Brown's Oscar HPC cluster. I investigated a practical question: **when does iterative diffusion sampling improve a model-based control pipeline enough to justify its added cost?**

I tested both halves of the pipeline—latent dynamics $p(z_{t+1:t+H}\mid z_{\le t},a_{t:t+H})$ and goal-conditioned policies $p(a_{t:t+H}\mid z_{\le t},z_g)$—using a pretrained LeWM/SIGReg visual encoder, TwoRoom navigation, PushT contact manipulation, controlled stochastic environments, matched deterministic baselines, and closed-loop planning.

The final answer was more useful than the initial hypothesis: **conditional multimodality alone did not make diffusion win.** Representation quality, prediction parameterization, training objective, sampling depth, and replanning cadence often mattered more. Several early positive results disappeared under stronger controls; those retractions are part of the project rather than omitted failures.

## System I built

The dynamics pipeline maps 224×224 images into 192-dimensional LeWM latents, then predicts eight future latents from three history steps and an action sequence. Its transformer denoiser has approximately 5.0 million parameters and supports cosine diffusion, DDIM sampling, and $\varepsilon$-, $x_0$-, and $v$-prediction. During CEM planning, each action candidate can be evaluated against multiple sampled futures using expected terminal distance to a goal latent.

A parallel policy stack maps latent history and a goal latent directly to an eight-step action chunk. It includes a diffusion policy, an MLP baseline, an architecture-matched Transformer-MSE baseline, action normalization, and a solver compatible with the repository's receding-horizon policy interface.

I also built:

- Lance-based latent caching and LeWM checkpoint conversion
- Frozen-encoder and end-to-end training paths with separate optimizers
- Synthetic-validated conditional-multimodality diagnostics
- Tunable random-door, hidden-drift, slip, and contact-noise environments
- K-step probabilistic proposer benchmarks and hierarchical-planning evaluation
- Shape, sequence-layout, checkpoint, gradient-flow, DDIM, and planner-cost tests
- Reproducible SLURM pipelines for training, ablations, and multi-seed evaluation

## A numerical failure mode in latent diffusion

The first diffusion model produced chance-level rollouts despite additional training. The cause was not model capacity: it was a numerical instability in the usual conversion from predicted noise to a clean trajectory,

$$
\hat{x}_0 = \frac{x_t-\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\hat\varepsilon}{\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}}.
$$

Near the endpoint of the cosine schedule, $\bar\alpha_t$ is tiny, so a modest error in $\hat\varepsilon$ was amplified by roughly 2,000× during the first reverse step. Implementing direct $x_0$- and $v$-prediction cut open-loop MSE from approximately 2.8 to 0.449 and reduced sample variance from about 8.5 to 0.4. This made diffusion rollouts usable, although it did not make them superior in control.

## Dynamics: sampling usually did not pay for itself

After correcting the parameterization, iterative DDIM still failed to beat one-step prediction or matched deterministic controls in the tested systems. Under extreme TwoRoom slip, for example:

| DDIM inference steps | Closed-loop success |
| ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 79.3% |
| 5 | 77.0% |
| 20 | 74.3% |

A replanning-cadence control reduced the one-versus-20-step gap from 5.0 to 1.7 points, showing that frequent replanning amplified the penalty. The defensible conclusion is bounded: **under these architectures and MPC protocols, additional reverse-diffusion steps added compute without improving control.** It is not a universal claim about diffusion world models.

## Policy: an apparent win that vanished under real seeding

The first goal-conditioned experiment appeared decisive: a diffusion policy scored about 41%, while an MLP scored 18%. I then found two confounds. The baseline used a weaker architecture, and the original training script did not fully seed the data pipeline.

After introducing an architecture-matched Transformer-MSE baseline and fixing Python, NumPy, PyTorch, worker, and DataLoader seeding, the final eight-seed results were:

| Demonstrations | Diffusion policy | Transformer-MSE |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Random-door / multimodal | 34.9 ± 3.6% | 33.5 ± 7.3% |
| Fixed-door / unimodal | 32.5 ± 2.8% | 31.3 ± 2.7% |

The original `+24` result—and a later provisional `+10` claim—did not survive. The final evidence shows essentially tied mean performance. Diffusion may have reduced training variance on the heterogeneous demonstrations, but it did not improve average success.

## The representation bottleneck

I designed a local conditional-multimodality diagnostic that standardizes the conditioning space, finds nearest-neighbor neighborhoods, removes a local linear trend, and measures residual structure with bimodality coefficients and one-versus-two-component Gaussian-mixture fits. Synthetic controls separated unimodal from deliberately bimodal targets.

In stochastic-contact PushT, the raw state transitions were strongly multimodal, but the frozen visual encoder largely removed that structure:

- Contact perturbation 0.5: residual-bimodality fraction `0.981 → 0.029`
- Contact perturbation 1.0: residual-bimodality fraction `0.978 → 0.064`

This suggested that a generative latent predictor cannot recover modes that its representation has discarded. The diagnostic also saturated on an observed-noise control, so these numbers are evidence of a bottleneck—not a general-purpose statistical proof.

## End-to-end training and a falsified mechanism

Fine-tuning the encoder initially collapsed latent geometry and planning. Stop-gradient targets and variance/covariance regularization preserved more structure, but planning remained weak. I then found a concrete BatchNorm mismatch: training used batch statistics while planning used stale running statistics, changing the same images' latent norms from about 0.14 in training mode to 3.55 in evaluation mode.

I implemented and tested a frozen-normalization fix. It eliminated the mismatch, but the corrected Oscar runs still did not recover planning performance. The bug was real; the proposed explanation of the end-to-end failure was not sufficient.

## Distributional subgoals: better fit, no control gain

A separate K-step proposer benchmark used episode-held-out data to compare MSE, Gaussian, mixture-density, k-nearest-neighbor, and diffusion predictors. Distributional models represented conditional support more faithfully than MSE in multimodal cells. Yet that offline advantage did not translate into hierarchical control:

- Sampled latent subgoal: 24.5%
- Mean latent subgoal: 26.5%
- Flat planner: 28.5%

Replanning and observation-grounded retrieval largely removed the mean-prediction failure the hierarchy was intended to solve.

## Limitations

This remains exploratory systems research rather than a benchmark result.

- The main training/validation split is random over overlapping trajectory clips, not episodes, so adjacent windows from one episode can cross the split.
- Most closed-loop starts and goals come from the same dataset distribution used for training; relative comparisons are controlled, but they do not establish held-out trajectory generalization.
- The PushT baseline used an incorrect block scale and a smaller predictor in much of the study, producing performance far below the published reference. Those absolute PushT results require a corrected re-baseline.
- The multimodality statistic is custom and primarily one-dimensional after local residual projection.
- Most conclusions come from TwoRoom and custom PushT variants, not a broad environment suite.

## Takeaway

The project began as an attempt to find where diffusion beats deterministic world models. It became a controlled investigation of why that simple thesis fails. The strongest outcomes were the parameterization diagnosis, matched baselines, representation and BatchNorm failure analyses, reusable planning infrastructure, and the willingness to retract positive results when seeding and architecture controls removed them.
